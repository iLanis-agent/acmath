AC Math

The chart on the box sizes a shady bedroom for two at eight feet. Your sunny kitchen with the tall ceiling was never in the chart - the sun, the stove and the guests add their share, and the yellow sticker prices a season that is not yours.

The size truth

The BTU chart assumes an 8-foot shady room for two people. Full sun adds 10%, a kitchen adds 4,000, tall ceilings scale the whole number, and every guest past two adds 600.

The power truth

BTU is the number on the front of the box; CEER is the divisor in the small print on the side. Divide one by the other and the wall sees watts the marketing never printed.

The bill truth

The yellow sticker prices a season of hours and kilowatts that are not yours. Your watts, your hours and your rate write the real day and the real 30-day month.

What it never does

It never sells a bigger unit, never calls a chart a promise, and never touches the wiring questions - the adjustments are labeled norms, and a licensed electrician owns the circuit.

AC Math is open source - the engine is small enough to read, and the math is plain enough to argue with.
